WHY THE MANUAL ROUTE FOR ZNB1 STALLS
INN354 - Accounts-payable process mining
Data: cases started 1 July - 30 November 2025 (log runs to 3 March 2026)
Scope: ZNB1 (manual order) invoices routed to the manual control queue CTRL1
=============================================================================

SUMMARY
-------
The manual route stalls because invoices sit and wait rather than being
worked on. Handling one takes minutes, but it waits days in the CTRL1 queue
first. The ZNB1 data shows six reasons for the waiting.


1. ONE PERSON HANDLES MOST OF THE QUEUE, AND THE LARGEST INVOICES GO TO THEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
- User1341 handles 31% of ZNB1's CTRL1 work and specialises almost entirely
  in ZNB1 (2,640 of roughly 3,100 items).
- Invoices waiting for User1341 sit about 8 days, whatever their value.
  Invoices handled by colleagues wait 1-2 days.
- 54% of the most valuable invoices (top quarter by value) go to User1341,
  against about 20% of the rest.
- As a result, the most valuable invoices wait longest: a median of 7.1 days
  in the top quarter vs. about 3 days in the other three quarters. That is
  the opposite of what the 2% discount calls for.
- A likely reason is posting authority: perhaps only User1341, or a few
  people, may post large amounts. Needs checking with the business.

  Invoice value    Share handled   Median wait,         Median wait,
  (quarter)        by User1341     User1341 (days)      others (days)
  ------------     -------------   ------------------   -------------
  Q1 (lowest)      23%             8.3                  1.9
  Q2               17%             8.3                  1.8
  Q3               25%             8.2                  1.1
  Q4 (highest)     54%             7.5                  1.2


2. THE QUEUE IS WORKED IN BATCHES, ON WEEKDAYS ONLY
---------------------------------------------------
- User1341 posts 28 ZNB1 invoices on an average active day, but up to 90 in
  a single day. Invoices pile up and are cleared in bulk.
- Manual postings peak on Monday (1,910), drop towards Friday (1,022), and
  almost stop at weekends (181).
- The 10-day limit counts calendar days, so a weekend uses up 2 of the 10
  days with no progress.


3. THE CLERK OFTEN CAN'T RESOLVE THE CASE ALONE AND HAS TO WAIT FOR OTHERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What happens after the queue                        Mean wait (days)
  -------------------------------------------------   ----------------
  Transfer to MM (purchasing must correct the PO)      9.5
  Sent for approval                                    7.9
  Sent back to data management                         6.9
  Posted manually                                      5.7

- In ZNB1 the "others" are often a single purchaser who placed a one-off
  order. That person has no deadline of their own for answering accounts
  payable, and may be busy, travelling or on holiday.


4. CAPACITY CHANGES OVER TIME
-----------------------------
- In July-September, ZNB1 invoices waited 7-13 days each week. From October
  the wait fell to 2-4 days, even though arrivals rose, from 80-300 to
  450-580 invoices a week.
- Backlog size and wait are not correlated for ZNB1 (correlation -0.12).
  So the stalls aren't mainly caused by too many invoices; they're caused by
  who is available and when.
- The summer peak fits the Norwegian summer holiday period. The improvement
  in the autumn suggests more people or new priorities.


5. THERE'S NO PRIORITISATION BY DEADLINE OR VALUE
-------------------------------------------------
- Nothing in the data suggests the queue is sorted by discount due date or
  amount. If it were, the most valuable invoices wouldn't wait the longest
  (point 1).
- The 10-day clock is probably not visible to whoever works the queue.


6. THE QUEUE IS SHARED WITH OTHER WORK
--------------------------------------
- The same people also handle ZJD1, ZJD3 and returns. For example, User1482
  handles 1,401 ZJD1 items vs. 959 ZNB1 items.
- When another type has a busy period, ZNB1 waits.


UNDERLYING CAUSE
----------------
ZNB1 invoices fail automatic matching more often, because they are one-off
orders with prices and quantities a purchaser entered by hand. Every failure
becomes a task in a queue that depends on a few people, works in batches,
and doesn't see deadlines.


HOW TO UNBLOCK IT
-----------------
  Cause          Measure
  ------------   ------------------------------------------------------------
  1              Spread posting authority, so high-value invoices don't
                 depend on User1341
  2              Clear the queue daily rather than in batches, and keep
                 cover on Fridays
  3              Set a response deadline for purchasers and approvers, with
                 automatic escalation
  4              Plan holiday cover for July and August
  5              Sort the queue by discount due date and value
  (root cause)   Send fewer invoices to the queue: tighter purchase orders
                 and correct PO references


CAVEAT
------
The patterns are from the data, but the reasons behind them (authority
limits, holidays, batch working) are interpretations that should be
confirmed with the AP team.

Definitions: "wait" = time from an invoice entering CTRL1 to the next event
on that invoice. Value quarters are based on Case_Invoice_Value_NOK (one
value per PO).
