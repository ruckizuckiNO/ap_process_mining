Why do ZNB1 invoices go to the manual queue?

At Initial validation, SAP sends each invoice either to the goods-receipt check (WAITGR, the road to automatic posting) or to the manual control queue (CTRL1). This report checks what the event log can tell us about that decision for manual orders (ZNB1).

13,289 ZNB1 invoices 45% to CTRL1 216 suppliers 4,720 POs

Short answer

No reason code

The log doesn't record why an invoice goes to CTRL1. The SAP fields at validation are identical for both roads, so the rule itself can't be read from the data.

59%

of CTRL1 invoices come from 39 suppliers whose invoices almost always go there. The supplier is the strongest pattern by far.

87%

AUC 0.95

A model using only fields from the log predicts the road correctly for 89% of invoices. The decision is systematic, not random, and points to a supplier or PO setting.

ScopeWhat was analysed

One row per ZNB1 invoice (PO_ID × SAP_PO_No), with the routing code (SAP_Next_Processing_Type) of its first Initial validation. Only invoices sent to CTRL1 or WAITGR are compared. The few routed via Warehouse Management (ZWMGRW, 110) or to deletion (DELE, 377) are left out.

The value is Case_Invoice_Value_NOK, which is the PO total, not the value of the single invoice. Shares below are the share of invoices sent to CTRL1; the overall average is 45%.

Same PO type, same process, opposite roads. Supplier361 sends 100% of its invoices to the manual queue, while Supplier115 and Supplier44, also large ZNB1 suppliers, almost never do. Something about the supplier, or the way its POs are set up, makes the difference.

3The decision sticks to the PO

Of the 2,413 POs with more than one invoice, 77% send all their invoices down the same road. Looking at each later invoice against the PO's first one:

  • First invoice went to CTRL1: 92% of the later invoices go there too.
  • First invoice went to WAITGR: only 17% of the later invoices go to CTRL1.

So the trigger is something fixed on the PO or the supplier, not something that varies from invoice to invoice.

Large POs go to CTRL1 more often, from 31% below 100k NOK to 84% above 10M. But the share rises step by step, with no cut-off that would point to a fixed amount limit. Later invoices on a PO go to CTRL1 more often than the first one. That could fit partial deliveries whose quantities no longer match the PO, but it's also partly the supplier effect: POs with many invoices come from the "always CTRL1" suppliers more often.

Amount differences don't explain it. If price differences sent invoices to the queue, invoices whose clearing amount differs from the invoice amount should go there more often. The opposite is true. The differences are mostly exchange-rate effects.

6How predictable is the road?

A machine-learning model trained on the fields above predicts the road for 89% of invoices on POs it hasn't seen (AUC 0.95, where 0.5 is a coin flip). The supplier alone reaches 0.93; all other fields without the supplier only 0.81. The decision follows fixed attributes, so it's very likely a configured rule, not a random or clerk-by-clerk choice. Setup, validation, field importance and robustness checks are in the separate report How predictable is the manual queue?

InterpretationLikely reasons and what to check

The pattern points to a setting on the supplier or on the PO, not to a check on each invoice. These are typical SAP setups that would produce it. They are hypotheses that the log can't confirm:

  1. A flag on the supplier's master record, e.g. a payment block or a "check invoices manually" setting that sends all of that supplier's invoices to a clerk.
  2. POs not set up for automatic matching against the goods receipt, e.g. manual one-off orders without the goods-receipt-based invoice verification flag. The invoice then can't wait for the goods receipt and goes straight to the queue.
  3. Price or quantity tolerances that hand-entered manual orders keep exceeding, especially for some suppliers and on later partial invoices.

Next step: ask the AP team or SAP support what is set differently for the "always CTRL1" suppliers (Supplier361, Supplier381, Supplier378, Supplier362, Supplier380) compared with Supplier115 and Supplier44. The blocking reasons and messages on a sample of their invoices would give the actual rule.
