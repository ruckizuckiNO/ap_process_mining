Invoices sent straight to the manual queue

ZJD1 and ZNB1 invoices that SAP routes to the manual control queue (CTRL1) at their first Initial validation, skipping the goods-receipt check: how they move, how long they take, and what they cost in missed discount.

3,304 ZJD1 invoices (32% of the type) 5,993 ZNB1 invoices (44%) limits 10 d and 30 d

Short answer

11.1% vs 22.9%

of these invoices take longer than 10 days in ZJD1 and ZNB1, against 4.3% and 5.6% of all other invoices of the type.

50% / 80%

of the type's missed discount (5.0M and 12.7M NOK) comes from these invoices.

83% / 87%

go from validation straight to Posted manually. The lead time is almost all waiting for a clerk.

Caveat

Only 1.3% of the ZNB1 invoices have a goods-receipt step, so their clock starts at arrival and any wait for goods is hidden. If each of these invoices had 5 days of unseen goods wait, ZNB1's queue would be no later than the rest of ZNB1 (section 6).

1Which invoices, and how the clock runs

  1. Selection. One invoice = one PO_ID × SAP_PO_No. An invoice is included if the routing code (SAP_Next_Processing_Type) on its first Initial validation is CTRL1. Invoices that reach the queue later (e.g. after an END_WAIT_GR on the goods-receipt road) are not included.
  2. Clock start. The same rule as in the invoice-clock report: the first Wait for goods receipt event with WAIT_GR, else the invoice's first event. For these invoices that is almost always the first event, Data management (arrival in AP); validation follows a median 1 seconds later (ZNB1). The few with a later WAIT_GR step (0.8% in ZJD1, 1.3% in ZNB1) went back through the goods-receipt check after a restart.
  3. Clock stop. The first posting (MM Posted Automatically, Posted manually, MM Autopost after manual process). Days = stop − start in seconds / 86,400, calendar days.
  4. Base and discount. Only posted invoices count. Late = more than 10 (or 30) days. The missed 2% uses the PO value split evenly over all the PO's posted invoices, the same rule as in the other reports.

Measured from arrival, both ZNB1 roads miss 10 days about equally often (23.7% vs 23.6%), but in different ways. The goods-receipt road is usually fast with a long tail: 2.0 / 37.1 days. Almost all of that is waiting for the goods receipt (1.0 / 30.0 days); once it's booked, posting follows almost at once (0.0 / 1.1 days). The manual queue is consistently slow with a short tail: 7.2 / 13.3 days, all of it time to posting, as there's no goods-receipt step.

With the invoice clock the goods-receipt road drops to 0.0 / 2.9 days (4.9% over 10 days), while the manual queue stays at 7.2 / 13.3 days (22.9%).

8Assumption: invoices come after delivery

Assumption used: suppliers send the invoice after delivering the goods. Then a manual-queue invoice has its goods from the start, the clock from arrival is fair, and its whole lead time is accounts-payable time.

What speaks against it: on the goods-receipt road the goods receipt is booked a median 1.0 days after the invoice arrives in ZNB1 (90th percentile 30.0), and 0.0 days in ZJD1 (90th percentile 22.0). So either some invoices do arrive before the goods, or the warehouse books the receipt late. If clerks waited for that too, part of the queue time would be goods wait.
