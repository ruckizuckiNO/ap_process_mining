How the missed discount is calculated, and how much the assumptions matter

Three choices sit behind every missed-discount figure for the ZNB1 manual queue: when the 10-day clock starts and stops, how a PO's value is spread over its invoices, and which value field is used. This report states each one, shows the evidence in the log, and tests the alternatives. Scope: ZNB1 invoices routed to CTRL1 and posted manually; deleted, never-posted and automatically posted invoices are left out.

5,386 invoices 1,724 POs 1,222 posted after 10 days 11.97M NOK missed discount

Short answer

Clock

Starts at arrival in AP and stops at the first posting. Arrival and validation are 1 second apart at the median, so the start event doesn't matter. If the real start were 1 day earlier (e.g. the invoice date), the loss would be 14.49M (+2.52M).

Even split

The log has one value per PO, so each posted invoice gets an equal share. 10% of the invoices are on single-invoice POs where the split is exact; the alternatives give 11.70M to 32.32M.

PO value

Case_Invoice_Value_NOK is used, not the clearing value. Per PO they differ by a median of 0.0% (85% within ±2%), and the missed discount with the clearing value is 12.03M (+0.06M).

Clock matters most

Split and value field move the loss only within 11.70M–12.03M. Starting the clock 1–3 days earlier raises it to 14.49M–28.70M, because 808 invoices are posted just inside the limit.

1The clock

Assumption. The 10-day discount clock starts when the invoice arrives in AP (its first event, Data management) and stops at its first posting, when it becomes payable. For ZNB1 the project starts the clock at goods receipt (first WAIT_GR event) where there is one; on the CTRL1 route that applies to only 22 of the 5,386 invoices.

Evidence. All 5,386 invoices start with Data management. Initial validation follows a median of 1 second later (100% within a minute), so starting at validation instead gives the same result. The 22 invoices with a goods-receipt step: 0 late with the GR clock, 19 with the arrival clock; using the arrival clock for all of them gives 12.14M (+164k).

What the log can't show. The log has no invoice date and no payment date. Payment terms usually run from the invoice date, which is before the invoice reaches AP, and payment comes after posting. Both make the real clock longer, so the figures here are a lower bound.

The clock is the sensitive assumption. 808 invoices are posted between 8 and 10 days, just inside the limit, so every day the real clock starts earlier pushes many of them over it: +1 day adds 2.52M, +3 days adds 16.72M. The headline uses the log as it is, which is the conservative choice.

2Splitting the PO value over its invoices

Assumption. Case_Invoice_Value_NOK holds the same amount on every row of a PO (true for 100% of ZNB1 POs), so it is the PO total, not the value of one invoice. Each posted invoice gets an equal share: invoice amount = PO value ÷ number of posted invoices on the PO. Missed discount = 2% × that share for each invoice posted after 10 days.

Evidence. On single-invoice POs the split is exact: 564 of the 1,724 POs, 10% of the invoices and 11% of the amount. The rest is an estimate; real instalments may differ in size. Never-posted invoices on a PO get no share, so the shares of the posted ones add up to the PO value. 468 POs also have posted invoices outside the scope (e.g. on the goods-receipt road); those take their own equal share.

The even split is the only rule that keeps the total right. Counting the PO once when any invoice is late (32.32M) or the full value on every invoice (227.31M) overstates the loss; splitting over all invoices including never-posted ones changes little (-0.28M). The uncertainty is in which invoices of a multi-invoice PO carry the money, not in the total.

3PO value, not the cleared value

Assumption. The amount is Case_Invoice_Value_NOK: the amount on the invoice at the exchange rate on receipt (course data description). Case_Clearing_value_NOK, the amount paid at the rate on clearing, is not used.

Why. The discount is a share of the invoiced amount and is decided when the invoice is processed, before clearing. The clearing value is also one value per PO (constant for 100% of ZNB1 POs), so it gives no per-invoice amount either. The differences are mostly exchange-rate effects between receipt and payment.

Evidence. Per PO in scope: the two are identical for 15% of the POs and within ±2% for 85%; the clearing/PO ratio is 0.977 at P5, 1.000 at the median and 1.029 at P95. Allocated over the invoices in scope: 2,899M NOK with the PO value, 2,906M with the clearing value (+0.2%).

The choice hardly matters. With the clearing value the missed discount is 12.03M instead of 11.97M (+60k, +0.5%).
