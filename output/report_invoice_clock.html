The invoice clock: ZJD1 vs ZNB1

How the time from goods receipt to posting is measured for each invoice, step by step, and how normal orders (ZJD1) and manual orders (ZNB1) compare against the 10-day and 30-day limits.

1How the invoice clock is calculated

The cash discount and the payment deadline both run from when the supplier's claim is due, which is assumed to be the goods receipt. They end at payment. The event log has neither a goods-receipt date nor a payment date, so the clock uses the closest events that are in the log:

  1. Data managementinvoice arrives in APstart if no goods-receipt step
  2. Initial validationrouting code set
  3. Wait for goods receiptobject code WAIT_GRstart: goods receipt
  4. First postinginvoice payablestop
  5. Payment runnot in the logreal SLA end, not measurable
  1. One clock per invoice. A purchase order (PO_ID) can receive several invoices, e.g. one per partial delivery. Each invoice has its own document number SAP_PO_No and starts again with Data management. The unit is one invoice = one PO_ID × SAP_PO_No pair; all events with that pair belong to it.
  2. Order the events. By timestamp, with Event_ID breaking ties. Item_No isn't used, because it isn't in time order for about 45,000 events.
  3. Clock start. The timestamp of the invoice's first Wait for goods receipt event with SAP_Object_Code = WAIT_GR. SAP logs this event when the goods-receipt check finds the booked goods receipt, so it is read as the goods-receipt date.
    • A Wait for goods receipt event with END_WAIT_GR (the wait ended without a goods receipt and the invoice was released to the manual queue) or with no object code does not count.
    • If the invoice has no WAIT_GR event, the clock starts at its first event (Data management, arrival in accounts payable). These are mostly invoices sent straight to the manual queue at validation. The log gives no goods-receipt date for them (see Is this clock fair?).
  4. Clock stop. The invoice's first posting: the earliest of MM Posted Automatically, Posted manually, MM Autopost after manual process. Posting makes the invoice payable. Later postings of the same invoice don't move the stop. The payment itself isn't in the log.
  5. Lead time in days. Stop minus start in seconds, divided by 86,400. Calendar days with fractions, so weekends and holidays count, as they do for payment terms.
  6. Base. Only posted invoices have a stop time. Invoices that were never posted are left out of every share and count.
  7. Late or not. Late means a lead time of more than 10 days (cash discount) or more than 30 days (payment deadline). Share = late invoices ÷ posted invoices.
  8. Missed discount. The value is only given per PO (Case_Invoice_Value_NOK, the same on every row of a PO). It's split evenly over the PO's posted invoices, and each invoice later than 10 days misses 2% of its share.
clock_start = first ts of (Wait for goods receipt, WAIT_GR) if the invoice has one, else first ts of the invoice clock_stop = first ts of a posting activity days = (clock_stop − clock_start) in seconds / 86,400 late_10 = days > 10 late_30 = days > 30 share_10 = Σ late_10 / number of posted invoices missed = Σ over late_10 invoices of 2% × PO value / posted invoices on the PO

With a goods-receipt step, ZJD1 and ZNB1 are equally fast: 2.2% and 2.2% above 10 days. Once the goods are booked, both post almost at once.

Without one, ZNB1 is much slower. In both types these are mostly invoices sent to the manual control queue (CTRL1) at validation. ZNB1 has more of them (46% vs 34% of invoices), they're late more often (25.7% vs 14.8%), and they account for 93% of ZNB1's missed discount. Part of that gap may be waiting for goods that the log can't show for these invoices (see Is the goods-receipt clock fair?).

CheckIs the goods-receipt clock fair?

Starting the clock at WAIT_GR instead of arrival removes the time an invoice waits for its goods, which accounts payable can't speed up. Two questions decide whether that's sound: is the event a good goods-receipt date, and can it be applied to every invoice?

1 · WAIT_GR is a usable goods-receipt date

Most invoices are posted within a minute of the event, and the next step is usually MM Posted Automatically: the event is the moment SAP found the goods receipt and released the invoice. The lag between the real booking and SAP's check is probably at most about a day (the median wait looks like a daily check), small against a 10-day limit. The wait before it is real: a quarter of these invoices wait several days for their goods, and one in ten waits weeks.

2 · But it exists only on one route

At Initial validation SAP sends an invoice either to the goods-receipt check (WAITGR) or to the manual control queue (CTRL1). Only the first road produces a WAIT_GR event. Share of posted invoices taking longer than 10 days, by route and clock:

The two routes are treated differently. From arrival, ZNB1's two routes are late about equally often (23.7% and 23.6%). The goods-receipt clock removes the wait for goods on the WAITGR route (4.9%), but it can't on the CTRL1 route (22.9%), because only 1.3% of those invoices have a goods-receipt date in the log. If clerks in the manual queue also wait for goods (holding an invoice until delivery), that wait still counts against them.

Consequence: comparisons between the two routes aren't like-for-like. "The manual queue causes most of the missed discount" is partly a result of the clock and not proven by the log.

Decision: keep the goods-receipt clock as the headline, always show the arrival clock next to it, and don't compare the two routes without this caveat. What the company's payment terms count from (invoice date or goods receipt) should be checked; the log can't show it.

4Assumptions and limits

  • Goods receipt is a proxy. The WAIT_GR event marks when SAP found the goods receipt, not necessarily when it was booked. For invoices without that step the clock starts at arrival, so any wait for goods before arrival is invisible, and any wait after arrival is counted.
  • Posting isn't payment. "Within 10 days" means posted within 10 days, which is needed to pay on time but doesn't prove it.
  • Invoice value is estimated by splitting the PO value evenly over its posted invoices. The real amount per invoice is in SAP.
  • Not the pipeline default for ZJD1. The other reports start the ZJD1 clock at arrival (the goods-receipt start is set for ZNB1 only), so their ZJD1 figures are higher. The ZNB1 figures here match the other reports exactly (1,687 late invoices, 15.9M NOK).
