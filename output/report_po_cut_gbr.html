If small POs ran like normal orders, how many would still be late?

The PO-value cut assumes that every invoice on a ZNB1 PO at or below the cut is handled automatically and posted within 10 days. Normal orders (ZJD1) show that this is too optimistic: some of their invoices are late too, more often on larger POs. A gradient-boosting regression trained on ZJD1 estimates the share that would still be late at each cut, and how much of the 2% discount would really be recovered.

1Setup

Question. For each cut (1.5, 1.75, 2.0 and 2.25M NOK), how many of the ZNB1 invoices at or below the cut would still be posted after more than 10 days if they were processed like normal orders, and how much of the 2% discount would they still miss?

Learning data. All 9,819 posted ZJD1 invoices (one row per PO_ID × SAP_PO_No) on 5,512 POs, July 2025 to March 2026. ZJD1 is the normal-order process with the same goods-receipt road that the cut would open for ZNB1. All ZJD1 invoices count, including the 32% that ZJD1 itself sends to the manual queue: that is how normal orders behave today. 2 invoices with a PO value of 0 are left out.

Target. 1 if the invoice was first posted more than 10 days after the clock start, else 0. The clock is the ZNB1 pipeline rule: it starts at goods receipt (the first WAIT_GR event) where there is one, else at arrival. An automated ZNB1 invoice would go through that wait and fall under the same rule. The arrival clock is a sensitivity in section 4.

Input. Only the PO value (Case_Invoice_Value_NOK, log10), as the cut is a rule on the PO value.

Model. Gradient-boosting regression (scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingRegressor) with squared loss on the 0/1 target. With that loss the prediction is the expected late share at a given PO value, i.e. a probability, which is exactly what the estimate needs. Settings: 300 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.03, at least 300 invoices per leaf. Predictions are clipped to 0–100%.

Monotone constraint. The model may only let the late share rise (or stay flat) as the PO value grows. Without it, gradient boosting follows the noise: the unconstrained version (grey line in section 2) jumps between about 3% and 25% for neighbouring PO values. The constraint fits the data (larger POs are checked more and wait longer) and costs almost nothing in accuracy (section 2).

Validation. 5-fold cross-validation with folds grouped by PO, so that invoices on the same PO are never in training and test at once. Uncertainty: 100 refits on ZJD1 POs drawn with replacement (bootstrap), giving 90% intervals.

Application. The 3,295 posted invoices on 672 POs of the 35 ZNB1 suppliers that go 100% to the manual queue (deleted and never-posted invoices left out), as in the exposure and cut charts. Today they are posted from the manual queue, measured with the pipeline clock (from arrival, as almost none has a goods-receipt wait), and miss 9.97M NOK. For each invoice below a cut the model gives its probability of still being late; still-missed discount = Σ probability × 2% × invoice amount (PO value split evenly over the PO's posted invoices).

Brier score = mean squared error of the predicted probability (lower is better). Brier skill = improvement over always predicting the average late share. AUC = how well the model ranks late above on-time invoices (0.5 = coin flip).

The monotone main model beats the average and the decile lookup table (Brier 0.0596 vs 0.0597). The unconstrained model is slightly better (Brier 0.0594, AUC 0.64), a difference of 0.4% in the error, but its curve is not believable, so the smooth monotone model is used. The skill is small (0.9%) and the AUC modest (0.62): PO value explains only part of why one invoice is late and another isn't. The overfit setting is worse than predicting the average, which shows why the leaves are kept large.

The model trims the simple estimate by 17%–20%. At the 1.5M cut, 1.06M NOK is missed on POs below the cut today. If they ran like ZJD1, about 4.7% of those invoices would still be late, so 0.18M would still be missed and 0.88M recovered. At 2.25M: 1.12M recovered, 0.27M still missed.

The cut alone moves little money. Even at 2.25M, 8.85M NOK (89%) of today's loss remains, almost all of it on large POs above the cut (8.58M). Raising the cut from 1.5M to 2.25M adds only 0.25M NOK of recovered discount.

4Sensitivity

Automatic road only. The model retrained on the 6,702 ZJD1 invoices routed to WAITGR (out-of-fold AUC 0.72). This is the road an automated ZNB1 invoice would take; the main model also includes ZJD1's own manual share.

Unconstrained model. The plain GradientBoostingRegressor without the monotone constraint (grey line in section 2). It fits the data a little better in cross-validation but swings up and down with the noise.

Arrival clock. The model retrained with the clock from arrival in AP for every invoice (AUC 0.70). This counts the wait for the goods as late time too, so it is an upper bound on lateness, in line with the project rule to show the arrival clock next to the goods-receipt headline.

The clock matters more than the model. On the automatic road the share still late is lower and the recovery higher. With the arrival clock, waiting for the goods counts too, so far more invoices would still be late and the recovery drops to 0.64M–0.75M NOK. The goods-receipt clock is the project's headline; the arrival clock is the pessimistic case.

5What this does and doesn't show

  • ZJD1 as a stand-in. The estimate assumes that a ZNB1 invoice below the cut would behave like a ZJD1 invoice with the same PO value. Different suppliers, goods and order types may behave differently.
  • One input. Only PO value is used, as the cut is a PO-value rule. The weak AUC shows that most of the variation in lateness comes from other things (supplier, goods receipt timing, workload), so single-invoice predictions are rough; the summed shares are what is reliable.
  • Goods-receipt proxy. The log has no GR date; the first WAIT_GR event stands in for it, as in the rest of the project. For the ZNB1 invoices it is assumed that the clock would start there once they ran through the goods-receipt check.
  • Value per PO, not per invoice. The cut and the model use the PO total; the discount uses the PO value split evenly over its posted invoices.
  • The large POs are untouched. The model says nothing about speeding up the manual queue for POs above the cut, where most of the money is.
