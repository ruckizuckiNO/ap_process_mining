Accounts payable · Process mining case · ZNB1 manual queue (CTRL1)

One clerk works the queue first-in-first-out, and that is where the discount is lost

Is the ZNB1 manual queue worked first-in-first-out? For User1341, yes, almost strictly. For the other clerks, no. Nobody sorts by PO value. User1341 posts most of the invoices from the always-manual suppliers, and almost all of their missed 2% discount.

0.94 age rank for User1341 (1 = FIFO) 8.2 days median wait 88% of the missed discount ≈ 0.5 value rank for every clerk

Short answer

FIFO

User1341 posted the oldest waiting invoice 94% of the time, with a median of 145 invoices in the queue. Same-day postings follow arrival order (τ = 0.87).

Not FIFO

The other 9 clerks score 0.23–0.62; several pick newer invoices first and keep waits short (0.2–6.0 days).

No value priority

Value rank 0.45–0.62, for most clerks close to 0.5 (User1341: 0.49). A large PO waits about as long as a small one.

8.63M NOK

of the 9.77M missed discount of the 100%-manual suppliers is on invoices User1341 posted (80% of the invoices). A strict, slow FIFO queue lets large invoices pass the 10-day deadline.

2How it was tested

Queue. Per clerk: the ZNB1 invoices that clerk eventually posted manually, from the moment each entered CTRL1 to its posting (7,572 postings by 10 clerks).

Age rank. At every posting: the share of the clerk's other waiting invoices that arrived later than the one posted, averaged over all postings. 1 = always the oldest (FIFO), 0.5 = random, 0 = always the newest.

Same-day order. Kendall τ between posting order and arrival order among one day's postings. Value rank. As the age rank, but by PO value.

Why it isn't chance. With a median of 145 invoices waiting at each of 2,638 postings, picking the oldest 94% of the time can't happen by accident.

3What it means

  • Sort the queue by deadline and value. User1341's queue takes 8.2 days at the median, close to the 10-day limit, and ignores value. Working it by discount deadline × amount would protect the large invoices without automating anything.
  • Spread the load. The other clerks clear their queues in days. The FIFO queue is a capacity bottleneck on one person, tied to the suppliers assigned to them.

Caveats. The log has no queue view, sort order or assignment data, so the queue is rebuilt from the invoices each clerk posted. The pattern is clear behaviour, not a documented rule. Assignment appears to follow the supplier, so each clerk's queue has a different supplier mix.
