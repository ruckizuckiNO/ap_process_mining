Invoice assumptions

The assumptions behind every invoice figure in the analysis: lead times, on-time shares, missed discount and exposure.

Event log: POs started 1 July – 30 November 2025, followed until 3 March 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026 with the course’s data description

1What an invoice is Definition

  • An invoice is one SAP_PO_No within a purchase order (PO_ID).
  • A PO can hold several invoices (ZNB1: 2.9 on average, 53% of POs have more than one). Each runs through the process on its own and gets its own clock.

The log has no explicit invoice ID. The course’s data description calls SAP_PO_No a case-level “purchase order identifier”, but in the log it varies within a PO (215,191 values for 186,588 POs; 15,425 POs have more than one), and Item_No restarts at 1 for each value. Every SAP_PO_No starts with its own capture (Data management), runs its own validation and is posted at most once; 93% of ZNB1 POs with several of them have at least two separate postings. About 6% of follow-up documents are resubmissions after a rejection or renumbering; the rejected versions are never posted, so each payable invoice counts once. Fits bulk orders delivered and invoiced in parts. The course staff confirmed (20 Nov 2026) that this reading holds and that we may define it this way.

2Invoice value Estimate

  • Case_Invoice_Value_NOK is, per the data description, the amount on the invoice in NOK at the exchange rate when it was received. It is one value per PO, identical on every row and every SAP_PO_No of the PO, so there is no amount per invoice document. It grows with the number of invoices (ZNB1: 1.6 invoices per PO in the lowest value quarter, 5.4 in the highest), so it is read as the total of the PO’s invoices.
  • The PO value is split evenly over the PO’s posted invoices. The value of a single invoice, and the discount per invoice, are therefore estimates. Totals per PO and per supplier are reliable.
  • Case_Clearing_value_NOK is the amount paid to the supplier in NOK at the exchange rate when the invoice was cleared, also one value per PO. The two differ mainly by exchange-rate movement between receipt and clearing (only 24% of POs match exactly, probably the NOK invoices); in total by 0.1–0.3%, so the choice between them barely matters.
  • A filter such as “clearing within ±0.5% of the invoice value” therefore keeps mostly NOK invoices and stable exchange rates. It is not a data-quality filter and should be described that way if used.

3When the clock starts Proxy

  • ZNB1: at goods receipt, meaning the first Wait for goods receipt step with status WAIT_GR.
  • ZNB1 invoices without such a step (the manual-queue route, or a wait that ended with END_WAIT_GR): from the invoice’s first event, its arrival in AP.
  • All other PO types: from the invoice’s first event.

The log has no goods-receipt date and no invoice date. The end of the bot’s wait step is read as the moment the goods receipt was booked (after long waits it fires on weekdays, mostly 08:00–12:00). It shows when the receipt was booked, not when goods physically arrived. END_WAIT_GR looks like a wait released without a receipt, so it does not start the clock. Arrival in AP stands in for the invoice date; invoices are usually dated a few days earlier.

4When the clock stops Proxy

  • At the first posting: MM Posted Automatically, Posted manually or MM Autopost after manual process. This is when the invoice became payable.

The log ends at posting and has no payment dates, so posting stands in for “could have been paid”. “Cleared” exists in the log only as an amount (Case_Clearing_value_NOK), never as a date.

  • Timestamps mark completions. Each event has one timestamp; the data description defines SAP_Processing_Type as the stage “just completed” and SAP_Object_Code as the “resulting status”. There are no start times, so the time between two events combines waiting and working time. Bot steps follow within seconds and manual postings about a minute apart, so gaps of days are read as waiting.
  • Each invoice document has its own clock: its own start (goods receipt or arrival) and its own first posting.

5On time and late

  • 10 days is the soft limit and the discount window, 30 days the hard limit. Both count calendar days, so weekends count.
  • An invoice is late when it is posted more than 10 days after its clock start.

6The 2% discount and exposure Assumption

  • 2/10 terms are assumed for every invoice: 2% off when paid within 10 days.
  • Exposure is the value of late invoices. Discount at stake is 2% of that.
  • Both are the most that faster posting could earn, not money the data shows as lost.

The log has no payment terms, so it is unknown which suppliers offer 2/10. There is no sign in the data that discounts were taken: the amount paid (Case_Clearing_value_NOK) does not cluster 2% below the invoice amount. Exchange-rate noise and SAP booking the discount on a separate line mean this is not proof either way.

7What is left out

  • Invoices never posted carry no value and no discount, as they were not paid. ZNB1: 902 of 13,776 (6.5%): 457 deleted, 376 sent to deletion at validation, 15 moved to a new number, 54 still open on 3 March 2026.
  • Robustness check: leaving out the 483 ZNB1 POs that contain such an invoice altogether keeps the on-time share at 86.9% and lowers the missed discount from 15.9M to 13.8M NOK (fewer POs, not more lateness). The supplier workbook notes/znb1_always_manual_suppliers.xlsx uses this stricter rule.
  • Cases without a PO type are dropped.
  • Observation window: only POs started 1 July – 30 November 2025 are in the log. From December on it only holds later invoices on older POs, so those months are thinner and not typical.

Open questions for the course staff

  • What does SAP_PO_No stand for? Answered 20 Nov 2026: reading each SAP_PO_No as an invoice document holds; we define it so.
  • Does Case_Invoice_Value_NOK cover all invoice documents of a PO (their sum)? It is identical on every SAP_PO_No of a PO and grows with the number of invoices, so we read it as the PO total.
  • Are payment terms (e.g. 2/10) and goods-receipt or payment dates available?
